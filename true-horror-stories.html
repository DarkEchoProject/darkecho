Three Stories for a Sleepless Night

A sound behind a wall, a reflection that lingers, and a voice from beneath the bed. Three original tales of things that should not answer back.

The room on the other side

The scratching began after the last bus passed the house. Mara knew the sound of mice. Mice hurried and stopped. This moved slowly, from the bedroom door to the window, as though someone were dragging a single fingernail along the other side of the plaster.

At breakfast, she knocked on the wall to show the landlord where it had happened. Three knocks. He looked at the wardrobe, then at the ceiling, and told her the building had no room on that side. Beyond the wall was open air.

That night she moved her bed across the room. The scratching followed. At two in the morning it stopped behind her pillow, although the pillow was now against the opposite wall. She held her breath. From inside the plaster, someone held theirs.

Mara found a photograph in the hallway the next morning: the house before the extension, a garden where her bedroom stood, and a woman looking through a window that no longer existed. On the back, in fresh pencil, were three words: Stop knocking back.

The last person in the mirror

Eli always covered the hallway mirror before a storm. It was one of his grandmother's habits, and he had kept it for the same reason he kept her teacups: some things were easier to preserve than explain.

One evening the cloth slipped. Lightning showed him his own reflection halfway up the stairs. Eli was standing at the bottom. In the dark between flashes he heard a shoe touch the next step.

He reached for the cloth, but his reflection put a finger to its lips. There was someone behind it in the glass, a tall shape folded to fit beneath the ceiling. The reflection was not warning Eli to be quiet. It was asking him to listen.

Eli covered the mirror and spent the night in the kitchen. In the morning the stairs were dry and empty, except for two wet footprints on the inside of the glass. One faced up the stairs. The other faced out.

The question under the bed

Nora dropped her phone beneath the bed and called it from the kitchen. In the bedroom, the ringtone stopped after one note. Then her own recorded voice said, very softly, 'I'm here.'

She checked the screen on the kitchen phone. The call was still connecting. She ended it. The voice in the bedroom kept talking, listing ordinary things: the coat on the chair, the book on the floor, the hand resting beside the mattress.

Nora had left the bedroom door open. From the hall she could see the coat and the book. She could not see a hand. She stepped backwards until her shoulders touched the front door, keeping the gap beneath the bed in sight.

Her missing phone slid into the hallway. Its screen displayed an incoming call from her number. Nora did not answer. A moment later, the voice beneath the bed asked a new question: 'If you're out there, who is lying up here?'
