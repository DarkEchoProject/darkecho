Ten Fictional Encounters After Dark

A countdown of imagined horror scenes, from an empty lift to a mirror that refuses to follow. Read slowly; the last detail changes each scene.

The elevator shadow

A woman stepped into an empty lift. The camera showed a shadow enter with her, though the doors had already closed. At her floor, she stepped out. The shadow stayed behind and pressed the button for her apartment.

The hospital curtain

The abandoned ward had been stripped of its beds. One privacy curtain still hung from its rail. When the explorers pulled it aside, they found only a patch of warm floor. Behind them, an invisible monitor began to count a heartbeat.

The forest reply

Lost in the trees, Tom called his brother's name. A voice answered from the left. He called again. This time it came from the right, closer. Then his brother's phone rang in his pocket. The message said: Don't follow my voice.

The doll on the shelf

The doll faced the wall each morning. June turned it towards the room before bed, convinced the shelf was uneven. One night she stayed awake to watch. The doll did not move. Every other object in the room slowly turned to face it.

The spare key

The locked door opened at three. A man stood in the hall holding a key, wearing the coat Ben had buried his father in. Ben could not see his face. The man hung the key on its hook and whispered, 'Someone else has the spare.'

The delayed reflection

She raised her hand. The reflection waited. She lowered it. The reflection raised its own hand and pressed one finger against the glass. A tiny crack appeared on her side, and cold air began to move into the room.

The final stair

There were twelve stairs to the bedroom. Simon counted thirteen in the dark. At the top he found a door he did not recognise. His mother's voice came from behind it, asking him to come down for breakfast. It had been midnight when he started climbing.

The empty shoes

Footsteps crossed the hallway each night. The landlord blamed the pipes until the tenant left a pair of shoes outside the door. In the morning they were worn through at the soles. Inside each one was a folded note: A little further tomorrow.

The baby monitor

The baby was asleep beside her when the monitor crackled. From the empty nursery came a soothing voice, humming the song she always sang. Then her own voice said, 'Don't wake him.' Her baby opened his eyes and looked towards the speaker.

The face at the lens

The night camera recorded a face inches from its lens. No body entered the frame. The owner turned the camera towards the wall, thinking it was a reflection. The next recording showed the back of the same head. It was looking into the room.

One ordinary detail, one impossible change

These are fictional scenes written for entertainment, not a list of verified recordings. Their shared device is a small violation of expectation: an extra stair, a delayed movement, a familiar voice from the wrong place. The related videos below explore the same kind of unsettling storytelling.
